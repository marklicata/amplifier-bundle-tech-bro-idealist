# Part 1: Product lifecycle stage

When identifying growth ideas, the best place to start is understanding
where the product is in terms of maturity. Use the rubric below as a
tool to help define what next-best-action to take for your product based
on its user growth and maturity in terms of PMF.

![](media/image1.png){width="4.173611111111111in"
height="2.620138888888889in"}

***Exploratory question**: what lifecycle stage is your product in? What
data supports your categorization?*

Identifying the lifecycle stage will narrow where to focus and what
north star metrics to watch when experimenting. After which, generating
interesting growth ideas is a combination of art and science. You should
consider both qualitative data (e.g., user feedback, UX studies) and
quantitative data (e.g., current product usage patterns) as input.
Ensure your ideas are grounded in user value and consider the
characteristics of problems your product is good at solving. Actual user
value is difficult to measure prior to any usage, but below are some
questions to consider.

***Exploratory questions***

1.  *[User value]{.underline}: what is the user problem the
    product/feature is trying to solve? Why is it important? Or how well
    does the product meet the user needs and JTBD of the audience(s)?
    Refer to the [Ethos and User
    Needs](https://microsoft.sharepoint.com/teams/AIMomentWorkingGroup/Shared%20Documents/General/AI%20Product%20Readiness_Excellence/Playbooks/Product%20101%20-%20Ethos%20and%20User%20Needs.docx)
    playbook for help with this step.*

2.  *[User experience]{.underline}: in support of user value, how will
    users experience the product? How do key features manifest to the
    user? What friction will the product/feature address or remove?*

3.  *[User actions]{.underline}: what user actions are critical to
    support the product's strategy? What should users do to realize that
    vision?*

4.  *[Critical user journeys]{.underline}: are there any linear journeys
    and funnels that are critical to supporting your user actions? Are
    there any notable drops between steps? Are any steps that can be
    made easier or more valuable?*

5.  *[Loops]{.underline}: what loops could bring users deeper into the
    experience and/or where users invite others to join collaboratively.
    For example, viral user experience loops and network effects driving
    user growth. Can specific features help bootstrap a new loop or
    amplify an existing one?*

## Understanding Retention

The ExP team wrote an excellent paper on the [Engagement and Retention
Improvement
Process](https://microsoft.sharepoint.com/:w:/t/CAICustomerSuccess/EcuchXuRctNCs0IQZFA1X9gBvr0c2Ec_uBX7Kd_Maui5kg).
The following are summary best practices for understanding user
retention.

- Improving retention is often more about **removing friction** or
  points-of-failure and **improving performance** than building new
  features.

- Very low early retention is often due to a **lack of
  product-market-fit** or an underdeveloped activation signal.

- Do not exclude any customers when measuring retention, and viewing
  retention across different **time slices** (days, weeks, months) and
  **cohorts** (m1, m2, m3) usually isolates growth inhibitors. isolate
  growth inhibitors.

- Ensure customer engagement signals represent **deliberate and direct
  user actions** and leverage A/B experiments to improve those input
  metrics.

- **Attitudinal signals** (NPS, HaTS, CSAT, etc.) can help generate
  problem hypotheses among engaged users.

## Ah-ha and Magic Moments

There is a subtle but important difference between "Aha" and "Magic"
moments within a product.

- An [**Aha
  moment**](https://productled.com/blog/how-to-identify-your-products-aha-moment)
  is when the user realizes value in a product and moves from user to a
  net promoter. An apple device prompting me to share a wifi password
  with another device attempting to connect. This is critical for user
  generated growth as these users begin to evangelize the product to
  their circles of influence.

- A **Magic Moment** is a critical step in the onboarding journey. These
  are key actions the user takes that makes them more likely to engage
  and be retained in the product. OneDrive prompting me to download the
  mobile app and back up my photos is a good example.

Sometimes Aha and Magic moments are one in the same, but often times
they aren't.

# Part 2: Iterate on ideas

## Define investments

Given the focus areas and signals from above, identify the top 2-3
investments which will create user value and contribute to the products
key metrics. For each investment, build a hypothesis that reflects the
actions you're trying to influence, how that action connects to your
business goal, and the metrics you'll use to measure that.

***Exploratory question**: what are 2-3 investments which will deliver
or highlight differentiated value? What behaviors will they drive, how
fast can you get signal, what is the hypothesis?*

## Find smoke

One useful tactic to product iteration is to get signal
quickly---sometimes called "finding smoke"---and sequence investments so
they build on one another. Some examples include "[painted
doors](https://crstanier.medium.com/a-product-managers-guide-to-painted-door-tests-a1a5de33b473)",
partial implementations, or hard code. The goal is to assess whether we
are (or are not) on the right path, not build perfection.

***Exploratory question**: what is the smallest investment you could
make with the smallest metric change that would indicate you "found
smoke"? Consider COGS, time to market, and what signals are needed to be
successful.*

## Balance your portfolio

Balance incremental growth opportunities (e.g., fixing funnel leaks)
with big bets (e.g., unproven product ideas) that is best for your
product. For big bets, the key is ensuring it\'s grounded in deep
product insights (and not just TAM) and is leading with user value and
experience.

***Exploratory question**: are your investments balanced across
incremental growth and big bets? Are you taking multiple "shots on
goal"? Are the ideas grounded in product insights leading to user
value?*

## Microsoft Plus

Microsoft Plus is an opportunity to leverage the ecosystem products to
amplify or accelerate your product's value prop and help it grow.
Alternatively it provides an avenue for your product to amplify or
accelerate other products. Microsoft Plus also has a set of accelerators
or ecosystem levers that contribute to growth. Refer to the [Microsoft
Plus Product
Vision](https://microsoft.sharepoint.com/:w:/t/TeamCTO/EYXlcuSfLsVOqWMEF7Ip0-sBdguVYjuZcSQ21KrAat95ZQ?e=4L3QR0)
doc for more details.

***Exploratory question**: how does the product/feature contribute to
Microsoft Plus? What ecosystem levers or "accelerators" does the product
leverage?*

# Part 3: Estimate impact

## Identify metrics

Based on your hypothesis, define what metric(s) will signal a change.
You want to consider both input and output metrics. The goal is to use
these metrics to assess estimated incremental impact of the investments
i.e., the impact we would not have otherwise observed without this
change. Refer to the
[Metrics](https://microsoft.sharepoint.com/teams/AIMomentWorkingGroup/Shared%20Documents/General/AI%20Product%20Readiness_Excellence/Playbooks/Product%20104%20-%20Metrics.docx)
playbook for help defining metrics and metrics ladders.

## Define a baseline

Start with the current value of the metric you've selected, such as how
many people click on a button or complete a specific task. This is your
baseline. Next define the best-in-class value for your metric. To
calculate this, you can use a close proxy, like an adjacent experience,
or an existing cohort of users engaged in this way. If there is no
reasonable proxy, you can swag it but aim high.

## Calculate impact

Calculate your gap upside---or estimated impact---by assessing the lift
you believe your investment will have on the baseline. This calculation
can be informed by previous experiments (internal signals) or by results
from similar experiments on other teams (external signals). If no such
signals exist, try calculating what a 1% or 10% lift would result in.
The goal is to calculate the 'best case' or 'ceiling' for expected
impact. It's ok---and frankly expected---that the actual impact will be
lower. You are calculating the maximum opportunity to be had.

# Part 4: Sequencing investments

As you look across your list of potential investments, use the rubric
below to evaluate investments across impact and cost. Also, consider how
easy or difficult it is to get signal. For investments that are high
cost, start to think about how your experiments will build on one
another. Starting with quick signal giving momentum to more complex
experiments.
